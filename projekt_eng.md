# Sales and Advertising Project: Nalehko

The project is prepared for the real company **Nalehko s.r.o.**, a Czech e-shop and retailer of ultralight outdoor equipment. The company grew out of the founders' travel and trekking experience. Since 2016 it has had a brick-and-mortar store in Liberec, and since 2017 also in Prague. In addition to the e-shop, it operates its own **Nalehko** product brand and offers equipment for trekking, long-distance trails, bikepacking, running and other outdoor activities.

---

# Assignment 1

## 1. Chosen company

Chosen company: **Nalehko s.r.o.**

The company is suitable for a Sales and Advertising project because it combines:

- e-commerce sales
- brick-and-mortar stores
- expert consulting
- community marketing
- its own product brand
- content marketing through a blog, gearlists and events

The company's focus is clearly distinguishable: it is not a general sports store, but a specialist in **ultralight outdoor gear**. The main sales idea is that lower equipment weight means greater comfort, less back pain and more enjoyment of movement in nature.

## 2. Product range and service offer

Nalehko mainly offers ultralight equipment for hiking, long-distance treks, bikepacking, running, ski touring and other outdoor activities. The range can be divided into several main groups.

- **ultralight clothing** - for example jackets, hoodies, vests, trousers, base layers, socks and accessories. An important part of this category consists of Nalehko's own products, such as Alpha Hoody sweatshirts, wind jackets and insulation layers designed with emphasis on low weight, breathability and practicality.
- **backpacks and carrying systems** - customers mainly look for low weight, comfortable straps, suitable volume and functional pocket layout. In ultralight equipment, the backpack is often one of the last items a customer chooses, because it must match the total weight and volume of the rest of the gear.
- **sleeping equipment** - sleeping bags, quilts, sleeping pads, tents, tarps and bivouac accessories. This category is very important for customers because it directly affects safety, comfort and recovery during the trip.
- **cooking equipment, food and travel accessories** - stoves, cookware, water filters, bottles, dehydrated meals, organizers, headlamps, first aid kits and other small items that customers need when building a complete gear setup
- **services and content** - the company offers in-store consulting, product articles, reviews, gearlists, route inspiration and guides on how to reduce backpack weight. These services increase the value of the purchase because the customer receives not only a product, but also decision support and experience

## 3. Six questions when searching for new customers

### What exactly do I sell?

Nalehko sells ultralight equipment for spending time outdoors. In reality, however, it does not sell customers only products, but mainly more comfortable and freer movement. The customer does not buy only a backpack, sleeping bag or tent, but lower weight on the back, less fatigue and greater confidence on the trail.

### Who is my customer?

The typical customer is a person who spends free time outdoors and cares about the quality, weight and functionality of equipment. This includes beginner hikers, experienced trekkers, bikepackers, trail runners, travellers and people planning a longer route such as Stezka Českem, the Camino de Santiago or a foreign long-distance trail.

### Why does my customer actually buy?

The customer does not buy only because the product is lighter. They buy because they want to manage a longer route, get less tired, travel more comfortably and have gear they can trust. With more expensive products, the effort to avoid a bad purchase also plays a role. That is why the expertise of the salesperson, reviews, personal experience and the opportunity to try the product in the store are important.

### Where is my customer?

The customer is across the Czech Republic and also in Slovakia or other European countries, because the e-shop enables distance selling. Physically, however, Prague and Liberec are important because the stores are located there. Online, customers move through search engines, outdoor forums, communities around long-distance treks, Instagram, YouTube, Facebook, Mapy.com, Strava and discussions around Stezka Českem or foreign trails.

### When does my customer buy?

Purchases are seasonal. Before spring and summer, interest grows in hiking, tents, backpacks, sleeping pads and lightweight clothing. In autumn and winter, insulation layers, down jackets, winter equipment and ski touring products sell more. However, the customer often also buys according to a specific planned event, for example before leaving for a holiday, a long-distance trek, a weekend in the mountains or a race.

### Who is my competitor?

Competitors are specialized outdoor stores such as Hanibal, HUDY, Rock Point or 4camping, large sports chains such as Decathlon, and also other Czech or foreign e-shops with ultralight equipment, for example the Czech store Pod 7 kilo.

## 4. Sales organisation

The sales organisation at Nalehko should be divided as follows:

The sales organisation must work in a connected way. A customer can start choosing on the blog, continue through a gearlist, ask by e-mail, come to a store and complete the final purchase on the e-shop.

# Assignment 2

## 1. Ten commandments for shop assistants and dealers

1. **Listen first** - first find out where the customer is going, what experience they have, what their budget is and what they already own.
2. **Do not oversell** - do not automatically recommend the most expensive product; recommend the product the customer will truly use.
3. **Sell the benefit, not only the parameter** - do not explain only grams and materials, but show what they mean in practice.
4. **Be honest about limits** - say when a product is not suitable for the given conditions.
5. **Use experience** - connect recommendations with real testing and examples from trips.
6. **Respect the beginner** - do not lecture a beginner, but help them find their way.
7. **Respect the expert** - give an experienced customer accurate data and space for comparison.
8. **Create a complete solution** - think about the whole gear system, not only one product.
9. **Support sustainable buying** - recommend repair, service or adding to existing equipment if it is more reasonable than buying a new product.
10. **End with clarity** - the customer must leave with a clear recommendation and understand why the choice is right.

## 2. Marketing mix: 4P and 4C

### 4P

**Product:** ultralight outdoor equipment, the Nalehko own brand, backpacks, sleeping bags, tents, clothing, shoes, cooking equipment, accessories and gearlists.

**Price:** medium to higher price level. The price is based on quality, low weight, technical materials, testing and expert consulting. For the company's own brand, Czech production and smaller batches are also reflected in the price.

**Place:** e-shop, brick-and-mortar store in Liberec, brick-and-mortar store in Prague, shipping through GLS, Zásilkovna and other carriers. The combination of online purchasing and the opportunity to receive personal advice is important.

**Promotion:** SEO, blog, gearlists, PPC, social networks, newsletter, workshops, festival, ambassadors, reviews, PR articles and cooperation with the outdoor community.

### 4C

**Customer solution:** the customer receives a solution for the whole trip setup, not only an individual product.

**Customer cost:** the customer pays more than in an ordinary store, but reduces the risk of a bad purchase, back pain, non-functional equipment and unnecessary kilograms.

**Convenience:** purchasing is possible online or in person in the store. Gearlists make decision-making easier.

**Communication:** communication is expert, friendly, practical and community-based. Experience from real use is important.

## 3. General sales promotion campaign

The proposed campaign will be called "Lighten the first kilo". Its goal will be to acquire customers who already go outdoors but do not yet have ultralight equipment or are not familiar with it. The campaign will offer a simple entry into the topic: the customer fills in a short questionnaire about their equipment on the website and receives a recommendation on where they can save weight fastest.
The sales promotion will combine an online advisor, blog content, short videos and a time-limited benefit. A new customer will receive, for example, free shipping above a certain value, a 10% discount on the first purchase in a selected category, or a free in-store consultation when buying a backpack, sleeping bag or sleeping pad. The discount should not look like a general devaluation of the brand, but like motivation for the first trial.
The campaign would run for six weeks before the start of the main hiking season. The goal is not only immediate sales, but also collecting newsletter contacts, increasing store visits and creating the first experience with the brand. Success would be measured by the number of new customers, questionnaire conversion rate, value of the first order and repeat purchase within three months.

# Assignment 3

## 1. Distribution paths

Nalehko uses both direct and indirect distribution paths.
The direct path runs through its own e-shop and its own brick-and-mortar stores. The customer buys directly from the company, which controls product presentation, price, availability, consulting and after-sales service. This model is suitable mainly for Nalehko's own products and for specialized equipment where explanation is important.
The indirect distribution path appears on the input side of the company. Nalehko buys part of its range from manufacturers, distributors or wholesalers, for example for foreign outdoor equipment brands. The company therefore acts as a retailer that selects and curates products for the end customer.
Logistics must connect stock for the e-shop and stores. It is important to keep key sizes and bestsellers available, while avoiding excessive stock of seasonal goods. Reverse logistics must efficiently handle size exchanges, repairs, complaints and returning goods to sellable stock.

## 2. Store plan

The store would be divided into several connected sections according to how the customer naturally moves through it. Immediately after the entrance, there would be an inspiration zone with new products, seasonal equipment and selected discounted products. Its purpose is to quickly present the current offer and also attract a customer who has not yet chosen a specific product.

After the entrance area, the main product zone would follow. The largest space would be given to backpacks and sleeping equipment, meaning sleeping bags, quilts and sleeping pads. These are important and often more expensive items where customers need to compare several variants and receive advice. These categories would be followed by tents, tarps and footwear. Clothing would be placed in a separate section near fitting rooms. Smaller accessories, such as stoves, water filters, maps or travel accessories, would be located in the back part of the store and partly near the checkout, where they can support add-on sales.

In the middle or in a quieter part of the store, there would be a testing zone. Here the customer could try a backpack with real weight, lie down on different sleeping pads, compare materials or have individual pieces of gear weighed. Practical testing would help the customer better understand differences between products and reduce the risk of an unsuitable purchase.

At the end of the store, there would be a consulting desk intended for longer consultations, for example when putting together complete equipment for a multi-day trek. Near it there would be the checkout and the pick-up point for e-shop orders. The pick-up point would also remain easily accessible from the entrance so that a customer collecting an order quickly would not have to walk through the whole store.

## 3. Equipment, materials and colours

The store should feel practical, clean and technical, not luxurious or overcrowded. Suitable materials include natural and durable materials, for example light wood, metal shelves, recycled cardboard for point-of-purchase materials and textile elements reminiscent of outdoor equipment. Colours should come from nature: green, grey, black, muted orange or blue. These colours support the connection with mountains and technical equipment, but do not overpower the products themselves.
Customers can be engaged in several ways. The first option is a weighing station where they can place an old product and compare it with an ultralight alternative. The second option is a "gearlist bar", meaning a place where the customer builds a gear plan and sees the total weight. The third option is a testing corner for backpacks, sleeping pads and clothing layering.
Extending the customer's stay in the store should not be based on random wandering, but on a useful experience. The customer should be able to read short practical labels, look at route maps, take an equipment checklist or ask about a specific problem. In a specialized store, quality time in the shop is more important than a large floor area.

The humour works because many hikers know the problem of an overpacked backpack. The advertisement makes fun of the situation, not of the customer, and at the same time clearly shows the benefit of the brand.

## 4. Project feedback

The project was useful because it connects sales, advertising, distribution, online marketing, CRM, PR and CSR through one company. The assignment is quite broad, so it forces thinking not only about advertising, but about the whole functioning of the company. The topic fits Nalehko well because the company has a clear specialization, an e-shop, stores, a community and strong content.

The most difficult part is estimating campaign budgets, because real prices depend on competition, season, creative quality and the settings of advertising systems. The most interesting part is working with personas and campaigns, because in outdoor equipment the difference between a beginner and an experienced customer is clearly visible.

Overall, the assignment is understandable, but some parts overlap, for example advertising, PR, online campaign and sales promotion. In practice, it would make sense to combine them into one marketing plan.
